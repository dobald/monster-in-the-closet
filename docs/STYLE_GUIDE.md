# Style guide: what the oni knows about putting outfits together

This guide is the research behind the oni's styling suggestions: the colour read, finishing touches, and "Got one?" prompts. It was compiled Oct 28, 2026 from stylist blogs, editorial guides, retailer style guides and fashion-design course notes.

**On the evidence:** most of these are conventions, not research findings. One fashion-design paper notes that empirical work linking garment composition to how people perceive it "remains limited." The app treats everything here as rules of thumb to suggest, never rules to enforce, and the user can always say "Not me."

## 1. Colour

- **Three colours at most, not counting neutrals.**
  - Black, white, grey, navy, denim, brown, tan, camel, olive and cream act as neutrals and don't count towards the three.
  - The 60/30/10 split comes from interior design: a dominant base, a secondary colour, and an accent. One example is a white shirt, navy trousers and a red accessory.
  - Some stylists prefer 70/20/10 or 80/20. Treat the numbers as a feel, not a formula. ([Inside Out Style](https://insideoutstyleblog.com/2019/03/using-the-60-30-10-rule-to-create-fabulous-colour-combinations-in-your-outfits.html), [Clothes Color Guide](https://clothescolorguide.com/the-60-30-10-outfit-formula-pairing-colors-without-overthinking/), [The LVLL](https://www.thelvll.com/blogs/colors/color-theory-for-fashion))
- **Harmony schemes.**
  - **Analogous:** neighbouring hues. The easiest to wear every day.
  - **Complementary:** opposite hues. High contrast, so let one colour lead.
  - **Monochrome or tonal:** one hue in several depths. The lightness steps need to be big enough to look deliberate, not like a near-miss match. ([color-analysis.app](https://color-analysis.app/blog/how-to-use-color-wheel-to-match-clothes), [Glamhive](https://glamhive.com/blogs/little-black-book/using-complementary-colors-in-fits-the-ultimate-guide-to-color-matching))
- **Neutral pairings that always work:**
  - navy with camel, the most-cited classic
  - navy with brown
  - grey with camel
  - olive with cream, beige or brown
  - Black with navy is the risky one: the two can just look mismatched.
  - Keep undertones together: warm neutrals with warm, cool with cool. ([Petite Dressing](https://blog.petitedressing.com/7-color-pairings-stylists-say-always-work/), [Inside Out Style](https://insideoutstyleblog.com/2024/08/combining-neutrals-how-to-choose-and-wear-them.html), [Westwood Hart](https://westwoodhart.com/blogs/westwood-hart/color-combinations-men-over-40))
- **Accents: an all-neutral look can take one colour.** Put it in a small piece (cap, socks, scarf, bag) and keep everything else neutral. The other way round, pull one colour out of a print and repeat it in a single accessory. ([The Vivienne Files](https://www.theviviennefiles.com/2024/03/looking-for-unique-accent-colors-start-with-a-scarf-emiliano-ponzi-scarf-by-echo.html/))
- **The sandwich, or bookend:** the shoes (or a cap) repeat the top's colour, with a different colour in the middle. This makes almost any colour combination look intentional. ([SheerLuxe](https://sheerluxe.com/fashion/trends/fashion-rule-book-sandwich-dressing), [The Everygirl](https://theeverygirl.com/sandwich-method-for-styling/), [Fashion Journal](https://fashionjournal.com.au/fashion/sandwich-method-styling/))
- **Value contrast:** light against dark reads crisp and graphic. Close values read soft and tonal. Head-to-toe dark can look flat, and a light break fixes it: a white hem, white socks or clean sneakers.

## 2. Layering

- **Thin to thick, short to long.** Lighter fabrics go next to the body and heavier ones outside. Each outer layer needs room for what's under it. ([Over Under](https://www.overunderclothing.com/blogs/whatscookin/how-to-layer-mens-tops-for-style-or-warmth), [Myer](https://www.myer.com.au/content/layering-clothes))
- **The three layers.**
  - **Base:** close to the body but not tight, such as a tee, henley or button-up.
  - **Mid:** a sweater or cardigan, thin enough to fit under a jacket.
  - **Outer:** long enough to cover what's beneath. A blazer can serve as the mid layer under a coat. ([Menswear House](https://www.menswearhouse.com/blog/weddings/layers-for-men/), [Bowties.com](https://bowties.com/blogs/the-gentlemans-guide/layering-for-cold-weather-outerwear-that-complements-your-style))
- **Peeks, not tunics.** An underlayer should only just show: a hem 1–3 cm below a knit, or a collar over a crewneck. It works best when it's fitted or close in colour. ([Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.at/en/page/your-layered-look-will-look-much-more-stylish-with-this-simple-trick-41118), [Inside Out Style](https://insideoutstyleblog.com/2009/11/how-to-layer-tops-2.html))
- **Patterns go lightest inside, strongest outside,** so the move from inner to outer layer is gradual. ([Over Under](https://www.overunderclothing.com/blogs/whatscookin/how-to-layer-mens-tops-for-style-or-warmth))

## 3. Proportion and composition

- **Balance volume.** Pair one relaxed piece with one fitted or cropped one. Over wide trousers, keep the top short or tucked: about a third top to two-thirds bottom reads well. Two big pieces together work only if something shows the body's line, such as an open coat. ([Petite Dressing](https://blog.petitedressing.com/7-proportion-mistakes-that-make-outfits-look-off/), [FashionBeans](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/wide-leg-pants/), [Ashlee Jaine](https://ashleejaine.substack.com/p/how-to-create-well-balanced-outfit))
- **Lines lead the eye.** Vertical lines lengthen. They come from an open layer, a centre-front placket, a long necklace or a tonal column, meaning similar tones top to bottom. ([fibre2fashion](https://fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/7724/dots-and-lines-can-make-garments-that-suit-the-wearer), [ISBE design notes](https://www.isbe.net/CTEDocuments/FCS-650164.pdf))
- **One focal point.** Give the eye one place to land, such as a print, a statement piece or a bright accent. Keep everything else supporting.
- **Shoes carry visual weight.** Chunky soles suit wide or heavy trousers; slim shoes suit slim lines. ([FashionBeans](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/wide-leg-pants/))

## 4. Texture

- **Contrast the finishes, limit the colours.** Matte against sheen, rough against smooth, soft against structured. A narrow palette lets texture do the talking. Denim dresses other fabrics down; leather reads sleeker; suede reads warmer. ([MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-create-outfits-by-mixing-and-matching-clothing-textures), [Petite Dressing](https://blog.petitedressing.com/7-ways-to-use-texture-to-make-a-simple-outfit-look-high-end/), [40plusstyle](https://40plusstyle.com/?p=111991))
- **Tonal looks need texture,** or one colour top to bottom goes flat. A ribbed knit with smooth cotton, or a leather belt on a wool sweater, keeps it interesting.

## 5. Pattern

- **One hero print, and one supporting role at most.** Vary the scale: a big print pairs with a small one. ([Harry Rosen](https://www.harryrosen.com/en/editorial/ask-harry-how-to-wear-multiple-patterns), [AOL / stylist](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/rocked-spring-most-unexpected-fashion-184307211.html))
- **Share a colour between prints, and treat stripes as a neutral.** A solid piece gives the eye somewhere to rest between prints. ([Beardbrand](https://beardbrand.com/blogs/urbanbeardsman/the-art-of-mixing-patterns), [Cultural Daily](https://www.culturaldaily.com/the-importance-of-mixing-and-matching-patterns/?output=pdf))

## 6. Formality: high/low

- **Pair every casual piece with one polished one,** for example a sweatshirt with a chain or watch. Or bring a smart outfit down with sneakers. ([M.M.LaFleur](https://mdash.mmlafleur.com/cracking-the-casual-dress-code))
- **Change the edges, not the core.** Casual outerwear or shoes over a tailored core works, but don't push the two ends too far apart. ([Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2017/10/highlow-dressing))

## 7. Accessories and finishing

- **Three accessories at most, with one dominant metal.** Silver suits cool palettes (grey, black, white, blue). Gold suits warm palettes (brown, cream, olive, tan). Match the belt leather to the shoes. ([MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-mix-metal-jewelry), [ABC13](https://abc13.com/jewelry-outfit-statements-how-to-wear/3009200/), [The Modest Man](https://www.themodestman.com/jeans-and-t-shirt-guide/))
- **Small moves.**
  - A front tuck shows the waist.
  - Pushed sleeves show the wrist and any jewellery.
  - A cuffed hem shows the shoe.
  - A "third piece" pulls a look together: an overshirt, scarf or hat. ([The Wardrobe Consultant](https://thewardrobeconsultant.com/blog/outfit-tweaks-that-make-a-big-difference-how-to-take-your-outfits-up-a-notch), [Leigh Williams](https://leighwilliams.substack.com/p/everyday-styling-tips-to-elevate))

## 8. The core most closets already have

Capsule-wardrobe lists keep naming the same core: a plain tee, a white button-up, dark jeans, dark tailored trousers, a crewneck sweater or cardigan, a blazer or overshirt, a coat, sneakers and boots. ([Classy Yet Trendy](https://classyyettrendy.com/capsule-wardrobe-starter-kit-35-core-closet-essentials/), [Broadsheet](https://www.broadsheet.com.au/national/fashion/article/capsule-wardrobe))

These are the pieces most likely to be in a closet but **not scanned yet**. That's what the oni's "Got one?" prompts ask about: they ask you to scan what you already have, and they never ask you to buy.

## How the app uses this

| Principle | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Colour schemes, neutrals, value contrast, warm/cool | **Colour read (配色)** on each look. It's computed from each garment's measured colour, not guessed, and it shapes the touches. |
| Peeks, front tuck, sleeves, cuffs, accessories | **Finishing touches (仕上げ)** from the playbook in `closet/styling.py` |
| Accents, sandwich, light break, tonal plus texture, high/low | Colour and texture plays in the same playbook |
| Capsule core | **"Got one? (探して)"** prompts: core pieces the closet has none of. "Scan it in" or "I don't have one". |
